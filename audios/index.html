Court Record  ·  Domestic Violence Proceedings — Cleveland Courthouse

Transcript — Testimony of Alan Carty

The following is a transcript of Domestic Violence proceedings at the Cleveland Courthouse involving Edwin Lewis and his then-wife Helen. The witness, Alan Carty, is Helen's father. He testified under oath regarding the events surrounding the separation and the allegations made against Edwin — including a deeply serious allegation that Edwin contends was fabricated. Edwin presents this transcript so that readers may weigh the testimony themselves and understand the ordeal he endured.

Witness 1 — Alan Carty (Helen's Father) · Examination-in-Chief

"The evidence which I shall give to the court shall be a proof of nothing but the truth. So help me God."

Court — Witness identified

Alan Carty. Resides at 12 Levitt Street, Redland Bay. Retired.

Q — Do you know the aggrieved spouse present in court today?

Yes, I do. That's my daughter.

Q — I'm going to take you to late April — specifically the 28th of April 1970. Do you remember that day?

Not specifically, no. I have so many things happen each day there, I'm not sure I can recall what happened.

Q — Do you recall what happened in April of this year, to do with your daughter and your son-in-law?

Well, if you tell me whereabouts — around the day they separated, I remember that, yes.

Q — When was that?

That was the 17th of March. It could have been.

Q — How long have you known your son-in-law?

About six and a half to seven years.

Q — Have you witnessed he and your daughter's family relationship? How would you describe it?

Most of the time I've been there they've been fairly happy. Different couples have disagreements from time to time — just an occasional argument, spread over a period. If you're referring to Eddie and Helen arguing, yes.

Q — Can you tell us what happened in the lead-up to the separation?

I'm not sure of the exact date, but I think it was on a Tuesday. Helen rang us at our house and told us there were a few things, so we went over to her place to see what the problem was. When we went over there, Eddie's parents were there, and we discussed different things being said, and we came home. Then on the Friday morning, Helen rang us again about eight or nine o'clock — she was very upset. She briefly explained what had happened between herself and Eddie. I said to Mum, I think we'd better go over and see what's going on. So Mum and I went, and we found her very distressed. Apparently Eddie had gone to work that Friday morning, and before he left he threatened her — telling her she was not to leave the house with the children, and he gave her other instructions too.

Q — What happened as a result of your going over there?

We decided there was no point in leaving our daughter in the situation we found her in. We were very upset and very concerned for her safety after he threatened her like he did. We told her to pack her goods and the children, and we'd take her with us. We packed her up and took her home, and then she went to see her solicitor at Victoria Point and spoke to her solicitor and barrister. After that, she and her mother got in the car and they went to Sydney for a fortnight to give her and the children a break.

Q — Did you have any contact with them while they were in Sydney?

Yes, my wife rang periodically and I spoke to Helen a couple of times on the phone. I think she left on the Friday night for Sydney. On the Saturday night, I had a visit from two police detectives from Cleveland — they knocked on my door and asked if I knew the whereabouts of my daughter Helen Lewis and the three children. I said yes, I did. They asked if the children were well. I said yes, the children were in perfectly good condition. I told them I knew their whereabouts, and if they wanted any further contact with Helen they should contact Helen's solicitor, and I gave them the solicitor's name and phone number.

Q — What happened then?

They rang my daughter Megan in Brisbane and asked that if Helen should ring her, she was to ask Helen to make contact with the Cleveland police, which she did. After Megan spoke to the police, she rang me. I rang Helen and told her to ring the police and tell them she was well and so were the children. When the police spoke to Helen, they said allegations had been made that two of the children were very sick. After receiving that, Helen took the children to a doctor in Sydney on the Monday, had the doctor medically examine all three children, and obtained a certificate that the three children were in good health.

Q — Have the police ever spoken to you about anything else?

No. The police told Helen there were allegations made against her about different things, and Helen told me.

Q — Who were the police officers?

Constable Mark Beardmore and his partner. They said they were tied up with the children's court, or something to that effect. I understood that they were representing the children's welfare.

Q — Did you have further dealings with these officers?

Yes. A few weeks later, I was rung by Constable Beardmore and asked to attend a meeting at the police station, which I did. Could have been the 28th of April — I just couldn't tell you exactly.

Q — What happened on that day?

I reported to the police station, roughly about three o'clock. Constable Beardmore and his mate took me into the office, sat me down in front of a camera, and questioned me — the evidence was taken on camera. When they'd finished, they thanked me and I left.

Q — Did you have legal representation?

No. I couldn't see any need for the expense of a solicitor. I was told what the questioning would entail.

Q — What were you asked?

The constable said, "A certain allegation has been made against you that you've been interfering with Justin Lewis, your grandson." I said, "Oh yeah — who made them?" He said, "The father, Eddie Lewis." One thing led to another — they were quite happy with what I had to say, and they dismissed me when I'd finished. They said nothing about charging me at all. They never said they'd charge me, and they never said they wouldn't charge me. They never mentioned a charge.

Q — Was this the first you knew of these alleged allegations?

No. I'd heard previously from my daughter and through different family members — my wife, all my daughters: Helen, Megan and Anne — and I discussed it with my son, Greg, in Sydney. The first time was when Helen called us over on that Tuesday, around the 18th of March. That's the very first indication I'd ever got of allegations of me interfering with this man's eldest son. I'd never, ever heard of the allegations before.

Q — How did the police interview make you feel?

How did it make anybody feel? Here I am, 64 years of age — and suddenly they've alleged that I've been interfering with a three-year-old child. For goodness sake. How would anybody feel? I was devastated. To think that this man had been told in November of the previous year that I'm supposed to have interfered with this child, and nothing was said to me or my family. Terrible. Goodness me.

Q — How would you describe your regard for the respondent?

I held that man in very high regard and treated him as I would treat any of my sons. I enjoyed many, many lovely barbecues at his place — he was a grand host. I was devastated to hear him saying things like that about me. I still hold Eddie in high regard, even though he said these things about me. But I'm so sorry for the man that he's carrying this on his shoulders like he's doing. Goodness me, that's a terrible load to carry. My feelings towards him are of great disappointment — great disappointment for me and for himself.

Alan Carty — Cross-Examination

Q — Isn't it true that you went to your daughter and Eddie's house on Wednesday the 19th of March, and that you were asked to your face at that stage if those allegations were true?

That's the first allegation I'd ever heard, yes.

Q — My client actually asked you if that was true, at his house when you were called over by your daughter?

Yes, that's correct.

Q — And you didn't answer that — you went outside into the yard and kicked a ball about, didn't you?

I did answer that. The man said to me, "Al, you wouldn't interfere with Justin, would you?" And I said, "No, of course not."

Q — When the police came on the 21st of March — the day Helen went to Sydney — they were just asking the whereabouts of the children at that stage?

That's right, yes.

Q — And it was later that you were asked to come to the police station in April, without legal representation — you felt you were quite capable to answer their questions?

Yes.

Leslie John Lewis. I reside with my parents at 1824 Craig Road, Cannon Hill. I am a contract mowing and general tidying up with BMD Construction. My relationship to the aggrieved spouse — she was my wife.

I came in through the door at five past six. Helen was up at the table with the two boys, and I saw that they were eating some yoghurt and some nachos. It usually has a thing with floppy feet on it that Helen got from the market. I just said, "Leave the children alone. Everything's OK. Just settle down." She placed Thomas down, got up and started abusing me — "You haven't helped me out, you've just been laying on the couch." Because she was going to take them to her parents' place, and they were fine in my care.

About 15 to 20 minutes later she yelled out that she had the police on the way. As soon as she said that, I jumped out of the shower, went and phoned my mother. I said, mum, I need your help — could you come down now. About 20 or so minutes after that, mum and dad came from Cannon Hill. By that stage, the police had arrived. I was upstairs with Justin and Clinton still in the shower. Helen yelled out that the police were here and to come down. I said no, I've got to turn the knobs off, dry the children, clothe them — I'll be down in five minutes.

Q — What happened when you went downstairs?

Five minutes or so later I went downstairs with the children — one under each arm, like supermen. They were loving it, having a good time. I said to the police — Adrian Staples and Caroline Cox — "What's the problem here, why have you been called?" They said Helen had told them this and that. They asked Helen if she had somewhere to go, and Helen said her mother lived five minutes away at Redland Bay. Then my parents came in, and the boys were ready for bedtime.

Yes, definitely. I rang Helen in the morning about a mowing job just up the road. She was quite OK. I said my parents were taking Justin and Clinton to Cannon Hill to get a change of clothes. Helen went berserk on the phone and said, "Your effing parents — they've interfered too much in our marriage. I'll be coming over and reminding them while you're out mowing." I got home at quarter to two. Helen was downstairs with Justin — I presume Clinton and Thomas were asleep. At about 3.30, Helen came down the internal stairs and went out to water the plants. I was going around the house trying to find a way to tell her about the allegations against her father. At quarter to four I'd had enough and I said, "Listen Helen, something's killing me here — there have been allegations raised by Kylie," my sister-in-law. Kylie and Helen were the best of friends.

Around Melbourne Cup time, when Helen was having our third child, Kylie asked Justin if he wanted to do a wee or a poo. He said, "No, no, no — you can't touch me there. That's where Poppy touches me there, and he hurts me a lot, especially when he sleeps with me."

No. I wanted her to ring Kylie and find out first-hand what the allegations were. But she replied back to me four times — yelling, "Why wasn't I told?" I demanded that Helen bring her father over so I could talk to him face-to-face. I said I didn't want her mother there — she'd just get in the way. But Helen insisted about seven times that she wanted her mother there too. They came over within five minutes. Helen's mother came in and sat on the sofa. He's never been left alone with the children by himself." Which I know to be incorrect — the girls always go out shopping together. Q — What else was discussed?

Helen's mother said Helen had organised counselling to try to keep the marriage together — that she was going to see Relationships Australia at Mountford, and had organised to see a psychiatrist. Everything was getting turned around.
