"The Lord is a refuge for the oppressed, a stronghold in times of trouble." — Psalm 9:9

Edwin Lewis

WorkCover & Justice

What follows is the truth about how I was treated by WorkCover — the system that is supposed to protect injured workers. I am laying it out plainly so that the world can see what they did to me, how the system operates, and why God has placed me in this position to expose it all.

"Speak up for those who cannot speak for themselves, for the rights of all who are destitute. Speak up and judge fairly; defend the rights of the poor and needy."
— Proverbs 31:8–9

The Injury

On the 3rd of March 2025, I did an injury on the job as a traffic controller. But I kept working — two weeks straight, 12-hour shifts, sometimes 14 hours by the time you lace up your boots, get out on site, and get home again. I did not stop. By the 16th of March, my leg just didn't work any more. My last day working was in front of the Surfers Paradise Surf Club, taking out a lane in absolute agony. But you work, and you try to keep the payments up.

The Toxic Work Site

The job site was toxic. My boss Danny was number three on the site — an unbelievable boss who knew what he was doing. But the night crew couldn't do their job. Every morning, Danny had to fix everything they'd stuffed up so the day shift could proceed, on top of his own work. With any work site, the first thing they have to take into account is traffic control before anything else — without that, nothing can happen.

One night the crew hadn't released the water out of the water barriers. Danny released it during the day and some people got wet feet. His superior — the boss above him — came over, and in front of about five of us, he dug Danny in the chest five times, pointing his finger into him, and said: "You are a disease. And you are infecting your workers."

I went to Danny afterwards and said, mate, I saw it all — if you need somebody to back you up, I'm here. But all Danny wanted to do was get out of there. The superiors above him and his leading hand were useless. None of them stood behind him. When I was going to get my knee treated and get better, I was planning to move to a different site. I had to get out of there.

The 7-Week Fight to Get Accepted

For seven weeks, I fought to get WorkCover to accept my claim. During that time, I went through three weeks of my sick leave and took four weeks of my holidays just to survive — just to keep the payments up while they sat on it.

I sent them ten documents — MRIs, everything — all proving I had done the damage at work. Plus, I was working seven days a week. And still, they were telling me they were "still thinking" about whether to accept me.

So I told them I was getting a solicitor. The following Monday — bang — they'd okayed it. Just like that. And straight away they wanted to know who the solicitor was. That tells you everything about how the system works. They don't respond to the truth. They respond to pressure.

The Doctors & Bone on Bone

Dr. Richardson told me straight away — Ed, the first MRI shows the damage. The second and third? Well, nothing's really changed. But the fourth one, after Dr. Metre had done the operation on the 16th of September — taking out two big meniscus tears — it's bone on bone.

And yet Dr. Metre wrote it up as only a 4% disability. Bone on bone — and 4%. My own doctor, Dr. John Nichols, could not believe what they had done to me. He looked at me and said: "What have they done to you, Eddie? What have they done with your knee?"

When I needed surgery last year, around May or June, WorkCover made me get a second opinion. They gave me a list of ten surgeons and told me to ring around myself. Why should I have to do it? I don't know any of these people. I don't know what I'm doing. So I'm ringing around, and I ended up with Dr. Metre, who just kept trying to let it settle. He eventually operated in September, but it wasn't getting any better. It was still the same problem I'd had from the start. And they just played me for the next couple of months.

It's All a Game — You're Just a Number

At Wally's wedding, 14 months ago, I met a man. He was the husband of Linda's — Wally's lovely wife's — best girlfriend. We got talking, and I took his number, because he used to be high up in WorkCover. A field leader — right up the top.

He sat me down and told me straight: "Ed, it's all a game. All you are is a number. It's all a game, and you've been played."

He told me what most people do — they try to go to Centrelink and all these other places, and they use up all their energy. They're just tossed from pillar to post. And then they're forced into taking their own life. That's what he told me. That's the system. That's how it works. And that's disgusting — to think that people commit suicide because that's the game they're made to play. That's the whole world we live in. Just corrupt.

I told him what they were doing to me, and he wasn't surprised. He said they're going to play you. And that was even before the doctors said what they said about my knee. He had no problems with me putting this on my website. He said: "Yep, no worries."

The Solicitors — All in Cahoots

WorkCover kept me on the system for nine months, playing me with physiotherapy and psychology, but they gave me nothing. They were ready to scrub me altogether. The whole system — solicitors, insurers, the lot of them — they are all scammers, and they played me like a game.

When I told my solicitors I was going to sue WorkCover for $20 million for what they had done to me — bang — straight away, WorkCover came back at me. But they came back at me through my solicitor. How did they know? They're all in cahoots.

Then this lady solicitor accidentally sent a document to me instead of to them. But that's the way the Lord wanted it. It showed me exactly how they operate — working together behind closed doors, trying to get me to play the game. They're telling me that if I say anything about WorkCover, they'll give me a rock-bottom offer. They can't do that. But that's how they try to silence you.

I do not want their solicitor any more. I told them: I'm not sending any more documents. I'm suing them now. They know it. I just want the truth to come out. And anyone who stands in His way is gone.

God Had Other Plans

My doctor still can't believe what they've done to me. I should still be on WorkCover. But this is how the story ends for me — I'm taking them to court for $20 million. That's nothing compared to what they've taken from me. I've lost my children. I've lost everything. And I've never done anything wrong.

But God had other plans. The same knee surgeon from the Mater who was ready to operate on me a year ago is now scheduled to give me a new knee. My doctor has the connection through Wally — his mother and father used to live next to Wally at Norman Park. He's going to make me the very first operation when he gets the chance. My knee is only a couple of weeks away from seeing the specialist. But the only reason I'm getting it done is my own doing. Nobody gives a shit. I'd still be waiting a couple of years otherwise.

Luckily, my physio was the one who told me to get a solicitor involved when he did. My psychologist has been taking it all down — she can't believe how I'm being treated. Everything coincides until the main factor takes control — and anyone who stands in His way is gone. "My God shall supply all your needs according to His riches in glory by Christ Jesus." — Philippians 4:19.
